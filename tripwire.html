How to use
Load / Save: Load a Stolperbeats .txt pattern bank file, edit patterns, then save back to .txt for loading onto the hardware via SD card.
Patterns: As on the module, every track has its own 24 patterns and plays one of them. The pattern library on the right shows them all: click a thumbnail to make it that track's active pattern — it plays and appears in the editor. Click a row number to switch all tracks to that pattern.
Arrange: Drag a thumbnail up or down to move it; the patterns in between shift along. The sort menu above a column orders that track by energy (number of hits, off-beat hits count a bit more) or similarity (patterns that differ in the fewest steps end up next to each other). Empty patterns go to the end. Moving patterns changes their numbers in the saved file.
Steps: Click a step to toggle it on/off, or drag across steps to paint. Kick and Snare have 64 steps (shown as 4 rows of 16); HiHat and Perc tracks have 16 steps.
Track length: Kick and Snare tracks can be set to shorter lengths (4/8/16/32) — the remaining steps are dimmed and always hold repeats of the loop, which is what gets saved (the hardware plays all 64 steps). Loaded patterns that repeat every 16 or 32 steps open at that length. Shortening a track whose later steps differ asks before overwriting them.
Mute: Click the M button next to a track to mute it during playback.
Choke: HiHat1 and HiHat2 share a choke group — triggering one cuts off the other.
Fill x4: For 64-step tracks (Kick, Snare) — copies the first 16 steps into all remaining slots (17–64). This overwrites whatever was in those slots, so use it to quickly tile a 16-step idea across the full bar.
Copy / Paste: copy, paste and clear next to a track act on its active pattern; you can paste into another pattern or another track. A 16-step pattern pasted into Kick or Snare loops at length 16; a 64-step pattern pasted into a 16-step track keeps its first 16 steps. Copy / Paste / Clear all tracks below the editor act on all six patterns shown.
Undo: Undo and Redo (Cmd/Ctrl+Z, Shift+Cmd/Ctrl+Z) cover every pattern edit, including moving, sorting, generating and loading.
Preview timing: Playback uses straight 16ths. The module's Shuffle, Timing and Feeling aren't simulated yet.
Generating patterns with Strudel syntax
Type a pattern in Strudel's mini-notation into the field next to a track and press Enter. It replaces the track's active pattern (Undo brings it back).
Any word is a hit (sound names don't matter), ~ is a rest. One cycle is one 16-step bar; Kick and Snare render four cycles, so < > can vary the bars. Hits that fall between steps (e.g. triplets) are moved to the nearest step.
Examples: bd*4 · ~ sd ~ sd · hh*8? · bd(3,8) · bd(5,8,2) · [bd ~ bd] ~ bd ~ · <[bd ~ bd ~] [bd*2 ~ bd ~]> · [bd*4, ~ bd]
Supported: sequences, ~/- rests, [ ] groups, < > alternation, , layers, *n /n, ! !n repeat, @n _ elongation, (hits,steps,rotation) Euclidean rhythms, ? ?0.3 random drop. Pasting a whole Strudel call like s("bd sd") uses the quoted part.
Sound and custom samples
By default, each track uses a built-in synth voice. To use your own sounds:
1. Drag & drop a .wav file onto the track label (e.g. “KICK”) on the left. The label turns green and shows the filename.
2. Samples are stored in the browser (IndexedDB) and persist across sessions — no need to re-load them every time.
3. To remove a custom sample and revert to the built-in synth, right-click the track label.
Mono .wav files at 44.1 kHz work best. Any sample rate or channel count will be converted automatically, but short one-shots give the tightest results.
